# Landing

The web introduction at `#/` and `#/about` features the first non-adult movie in TMDb's weekly movie ranking, not the mixed feed or popularity sorting. Details provide its logo, synopsis, year, runtime, genres, rating, cast and up to five available backdrop stills. Missing data stays omitted; unavailable weekly data exposes Retry rather than labeling bundled titles as trending.

The trailer uses YouTube's privacy-enhanced iframe embed, autoplaying muted where permitted. A sound toggle and manual Play trailer handle browser autoplay restrictions. A second muted, non-interactive iframe shows the same trailer behind the page with blur; trusted foreground playback events synchronize it, and after fourteen seconds it fades into the featured movie's blurred fixed backdrop. This approximates the film's palette through matching artwork; it does not extract YouTube frames or guarantee frame-exact color matching. Background playback pauses after the fade or when the trailer leaves the viewport. No trailer means a still-image feature, not an invented video.

Trailers, RainFlix and More to watch buttons smooth-scroll within the page without changing hash routes. Desktop retains a compact sticky pill; mobile uses a full-width sticky row with three equal columns, without rounded enclosure, respecting safe-area insets. Content and the rounded trailer have small 4% side margins. The featured logo and capsule download action are stacked below the trailer, left-aligned on desktop and centered on mobile. The weekly-trending caption is omitted. Other landing actions also use capsule shapes. The trailer maintains a 16:9 container without a mismatched desktop height cap, and its iframe fills that box. YouTube/source-internal letterboxing cannot be styled from the parent page. The regular mobile web catalog retains bottom navigation, with Search remaining in its top header and no drawer. Native apps still start at Home.

Three feature cards precede two carousels: mixed weekly trending titles, then interleaved weekly anime and Korean dramas. Anime uses the existing filtered feed; K-dramas are Korean-language drama series from the first three weekly TV ranking pages. Coverage is limited to these candidates. Empty feeds show a quiet empty state. Carousels loop with decorative duplicates and slowly advance while visible; arrows, touch scrolling, keyboard arrows and mouse dragging allow manual navigation. Interaction briefly yields to manual scrolling, then resumes. Clicking a title never permanently pauses animation. Links open the built-in title page in a new tab.

Download RainFlix and Open RainFlix on web open new tabs. Watch now opens Downloads in a new tab, not the featured title. It is hidden during the initial trailer/details view, sliding up as the What is RainFlix section enters the viewing area after scrolling. Its full-width bottom panel uses a broad 90px masked blur above a near-black base, fading into content without a hard rectangular upper edge, then settles into the final call-to-action. Hidden panels are inert and excluded from assistive navigation. The first carousel moves left-to-right; the anime/K-drama row moves right-to-left. Manual arrows retain their conventional directions. The About action points to the owner's requested repository README; implementation does not publish any source or releases.
